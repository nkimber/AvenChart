From generation to evaluation

A deliberate pause to understand the code.

Phase 1 established that coding agents could produce a broad, functioning reimplementation of a mature healthcare application. Phase 2 asks a different question: is the result understandable, safe, maintainable, efficient, and structurally sound enough to justify continued investment?

Phase 1 is now historical. Its source revision, measurements, and approximately 86% functional-coverage estimate are fixed and will not be revised as later work changes the application.

Phase 01 · Experimental build · Closed

Can an agent rebuild a mature application?

AvenChart tested whether autonomous coding agents could recreate and modernize the behavior of an established EHR and practice-management product on a new technology stack. OpenEMR supplied the functional benchmark; the retained AvenChart application code was produced by coding agents.

This was a functional reimplementation, not a line-by-line translation. OpenEMR source was used to understand product behavior and workflows, not as code to port.

Estimated functional coverage at closure was approximately 86%. Most of the remaining gap was concentrated in security capabilities and external integrations. This estimate is retained as a Phase 1 observation, not a certification of correctness or completeness.

The experiment

Generation came before evaluation.

The Phase 1 objective was broad functional reconstruction, one behavior slice at a time, under an unusual autonomy constraint.

OpenEMR was selected because it is a mature, extensively used open-source EHR and practice-management application. Its breadth and real-world functionality made the autonomy test demanding enough to be meaningful.

Core experimental constraint Zero human-authored application code.

The human role was limited to setting direction and objectives. That constraint makes Phase 1 useful as an experiment, but it also means the output now requires deliberate human-oriented critique before it can be trusted as an engineering foundation.

Generation pass 01

Functional baseline

Agents created an ASP.NET Core API, PostgreSQL data model, migrations, verification scripts, and a utilitarian React reference interface while working through OpenEMR functionality slice by slice.

Generation pass 02

Interface modernization

A second agent produced a more polished React and TypeScript clinician and portal interface against the same API and data foundation. Both generation passes belong to the Phase 1 experiment.

What Phase 1 does not claim

Functional breadth does not establish architectural quality, clinical safety, privacy, security, performance, maintainability, accessibility, regulatory readiness, or production fitness. Those are explicit subjects of Phase 2, not conclusions imported into this historical record.

    Phase charter

    A decision phase, not a refactoring sprint.

    The purpose is to replace impressions with a traceable account of the codebase’s strengths, risks, liabilities, and opportunities.

    Primary question
    Is this a sound foundation for a maintainable healthcare product?
    Entry condition
    Phase 1 source and measurements fixed at one revision
    In scope
    The complete solution, its runtime behavior, tests, data, operations, and documentation
    Out of scope
    Unapproved modernization work and claims of production readiness
    Exit condition
    Coverage is complete, findings are validated, and recommendations are ordered and accepted
    Decision output
    Proceed, proceed conditionally, reduce scope, replace components, or stop

    The assessment must leave a usable body of evidence.

    Assessment flow

    From inventory to an approved roadmap.

    1. 01

      Inventory

      Map projects, modules, routes, persistence, integrations, tests, and runtime boundaries.

    2. 02

      Assess

      Apply the same quality model to every subsystem and record both evidence and uncertainty.

    3. 03

      Validate

      Confirm important findings through tests, traces, measurements, and cross-component review.

    4. 04

      Recommend

      Describe target states and measurable benefits, including cost, prerequisites, and alternatives.

    5. 05

      Decide

      Accept, defer, reject, or combine recommendations and approve the Phase 3 sequence.

    Operating principles

    Evidence before preference.

    • Assess the system that exists, not the architecture its names imply.
    • Separate production blockers from ordinary maintainability and style concerns.
    • Distinguish isolated defects from repeated patterns and systemic causes.
    • Do not recommend a fashionable pattern without a demonstrated problem and measurable benefit.
    • Record uncertainty explicitly when evidence is incomplete or behavior cannot yet be reproduced.

    Evidence standard

    Every conclusion must be traceable.

    Observe

    Concrete condition

    Name the behavior, structure, or absence that was actually found.

    Locate

    Reproducible evidence

    Link the code, configuration, trace, query plan, test, or measured scenario.

    Interpret

    Consequence and reach

    Explain why it matters, who or what is affected, and whether it is isolated or systemic.

    Calibrate

    Confidence and uncertainty

    Record what supports the conclusion and what remains unknown or untested.

    Finding severity

    Priority reflects consequence, not annoyance.

    Severity combines plausible impact, likelihood, affected population, detectability, and blast radius. Effort is recorded separately so a difficult problem is not mistaken for a low-priority problem.

    Blocker

    Could make clinical or production use unacceptable; must be resolved or explicitly accepted before release.

    High

    Material correctness, security, privacy, reliability, or systemic maintainability risk requiring early action.

    Medium

    Meaningful localized risk or recurring cost that should be planned but does not independently block all progress.

    Low

    Contained defect, inconsistency, or inefficiency with limited consequence and straightforward workarounds.

    Opportunity

    Optional improvement with measurable value but no demonstrated defect or unmanaged risk.

    Completion rule

    “Reviewed” must mean something.

    • All in-scope subsystems are mapped to every applicable assessment domain.
    • Each coverage item records evidence, status, reviewer, date, and unresolved uncertainty.
    • High-severity and systemic findings have been independently reproduced or corroborated.
    • Excluded areas are named with a reason, risk statement, and follow-up decision.
    • The findings register and scorecard agree with the underlying evidence.

    Triage policy

    Substance survives; noise does not set the agenda.

    Value is determined by specificity, relevance, consequence, and falsifiability—not sentiment, votes, confidence, or whether the project agrees with the commenter.

    Retain

    Testable criticism

    Specific code, behavior, evidence, process, risk, or cost claims that would matter if true.

    Narrow

    Over-broad but useful

    Remove rhetoric, split absolute claims into answerable questions, and merge comments that share one cause.

    Exclude

    Low-value material

    Generic praise or abuse, unsupported prediction, factual misunderstanding, promotion, and duplicates—with the reason retained.

    Agent boundary

    Criticism advocates, not commenter personas.

    • Existing Phase 2 specialists represent the strongest fair version of a sourced technical argument.
    • They seek counterexamples, strengths, compensating controls, and evidence that would falsify the concern.
    • They do not impersonate public users, mine unrelated history, or infer motives, personality, employment, expertise, or sensitive traits.
    • Overlapping comments become one bounded packet; no more than three specialists run concurrently.
    • External challenge IDs remain intake references and never replace canonical findings or recommendations.

    Finding record

    One schema for every quality domain.

    Consistent fields make findings comparable, searchable, reviewable, and suitable for turning into recommendations without losing the evidence that justified them.

    FieldPurposeRequired content
    Identifier and titleStable referenceDomain prefix, sequence, and a precise condition—not a proposed fix
    ConditionWhat existsThe behavior, structure, omission, or repeated pattern observed
    EvidenceReproducibilityFiles, lines, tests, traces, measurements, requests, plans, or screenshots
    ConsequenceWhy it mattersImpact on users, patients, data, operations, delivery, or future change
    Cause and reachSystem contextLikely cause, affected components, frequency, and isolated versus systemic classification
    RiskPriority inputSeverity, likelihood, blast radius, detectability, confidence, and production-blocker status
    DispositionGovernanceOpen, validated, disputed, accepted risk, addressed, superseded, or closed

    Finding lifecycle

    From observation to accepted evidence.

    1. Observed

      A potential condition is captured with its initial location and context.

    2. Reproduced

      The condition is confirmed through a second path, executable scenario, or corroborating evidence.

    3. Analyzed

      Cause, reach, consequence, severity, confidence, and uncertainty are recorded.

    4. Validated

      The finding is reviewed for accuracy, duplication, proportionality, and systemic implications.

    5. Dispositioned

      It is linked to a recommendation, accepted as risk, deferred, disputed, or closed with rationale.

    Analytical boundary

    Context is not a finding.

    The approximately 86% coverage estimate, the GenAI-only creation method, and known functional gaps explain why the assessment exists. They do not prove that any particular architecture, subsystem, or implementation is poor.

    Likewise, modern syntax, an ORM, a test suite, or a named architectural pattern does not prove quality. Phase 2 evaluates whether each choice works in this system and whether its actual consequences are acceptable.

    Phase 02 · Recommendations

    Turn findings into an ordered decision.

    A recommendation describes a justified change in direction or target state. It must identify the problem it addresses, the value it creates, the evidence that will prove success, and the dependencies and risks that determine when—or whether—it should be implemented.

    Recommendation record

    A proposal must explain more than the desired technology.

    Recommendations are written so they can be accepted, deferred, rejected, combined, or implemented without reconstructing the original argument from memory.

    Problem

    Linked findings

    The validated conditions, risks, and root causes this proposal addresses.

    Direction

    Target state

    The intended structure or behavior, including what will remain unchanged.

    Value

    Expected benefit

    Risk reduction, correctness, safety, performance, clarity, cost, or delivery improvement.

    Sequence

    Dependencies

    Prerequisites, unlocked work, conflicts, migration order, and decisions that must come first.

    Effort

    Size and complexity

    Estimated breadth, technical difficulty, coordination, migration needs, and specialist involvement.

    Risk

    Change exposure

    Regression surface, data risk, operational impact, reversibility, and rollout strategy.

    Proof

    Acceptance criteria

    Observable outcomes and tests that distinguish completion from partial implementation.

    Choice

    Alternatives and disposition

    Viable alternatives, do-nothing consequences, and the accept, defer, reject, or combine decision.

    Default ordering logic

    Protect the system before polishing it.

    The final roadmap will follow actual dependencies, but it begins with a risk-first bias so attractive cleanup does not displace safety, correctness, security, privacy, or recoverability work.

    1. 01

      Production and safety blockers

      Conditions that make deployment or clinical reliance unacceptable.

    2. 02

      High-risk correctness, security, and privacy

      Material exposure that can be addressed without waiting for broader redesign.

    3. 03

      Foundational architecture and boundaries

      Changes that reduce systemic coupling and make later work safer and smaller.

    4. 04

      Data, performance, and operational resilience

      Measured bottlenecks, consistency risks, failure recovery, and capacity constraints.

    5. 05

      Maintainability and duplication

      Repeated implementation cost, excessive bulk, unclear ownership, and difficult change paths.

    6. 06

      Developer experience and stylistic consistency

      Tooling, conventions, documentation, and refinements that compound future delivery quality.

    Delivery principles

    Improve without losing the ability to explain why.

    Progress is defined by accepted outcomes and retired risk, not lines changed, technologies introduced, or the number of recommendations marked complete.

    Traceability

    Findings stay attached

    Every change identifies its recommendation, affected findings, acceptance criteria, and evidence.

    Incrementality

    Smallest valuable slice

    Large target states are decomposed into reversible milestones that leave the system coherent.

    Behavior

    Required outcomes remain protected

    Functional contracts, data meaning, and user workflows change only when the recommendation explicitly requires it.

    Measurement

    Before and after are comparable

    Performance, reliability, complexity, and operational claims use agreed baselines and repeatable measurements.

    Migration

    Transitions are first-class work

    Data, APIs, configuration, deployments, and rollback receive the same design attention as the end state.

    Restraint

    No architecture for its own sake

    New abstractions, services, libraries, or patterns must remove a demonstrated problem at acceptable cost.

    Phase 03 · Quality gates

    Completion requires proof, not confidence.

    Quality gates define the minimum evidence a change must produce before it can advance. The exact tests and measurements vary by recommendation, but no delivery can waive relevant safety, data, security, migration, or operational concerns by declaring them out of scope after implementation begins.

    02

    Correctness

    Do focused and end-to-end tests protect required behavior, invariants, and failure cases?

    03

    Safety and security

    Have relevant clinical, privacy, authorization, misuse, and sensitive-data risks been reviewed?

    04

    Data and migration

    Are schema, compatibility, conversion, concurrency, rollback, retention, and recovery demonstrated?

    05

    Performance

    Are affected latency, throughput, resource, query, and capacity claims measured against the baseline?

    06

    Operations

    Can the change be observed, deployed, supported, diagnosed, rolled back, and recovered safely?

    07

    Maintainability

    Are ownership, documentation, architecture records, tests, and future change paths clearer than before?
